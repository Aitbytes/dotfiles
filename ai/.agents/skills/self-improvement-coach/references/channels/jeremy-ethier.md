# Jeremy Ethier — Channel Deep Dive

## Channel Overview

**Channel:** [Jeremy Ethier](https://www.youtube.com/@JeremyEthier)  
**Founder:** Jeremy Ethier — Kinesiologist, CSCS, founder of Built With Science  
**Credentials:** BSc in Kinesiology, Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist

Jeremy Ethier built his channel on a simple formula: **take one fitness question, find all the relevant research, synthesize the answer with clear visuals, and provide an actionable plan.** His content is notably systematic — every video follows a research → explanation → practical application structure. He's distinct from other science-based creators in his focus on biomechanics and EMG (electromyography) research to determine optimal exercise technique. His Built With Science programs emphasize full-body training 3×/week as the most efficient approach for the majority of people.

### What Makes Jeremy Ethier Unique

- **EMG-driven exercise selection** — He heavily relies on electromyography studies to determine which exercises maximally activate target muscles, then tests variations to find the optimal technique.
- **"Activation" vs. "Effectiveness" distinction** — He's careful to note that high EMG activation doesn't automatically mean an exercise is best for growth; other factors matter (loading potential, joint stress, progressive overload capacity).
- **Full-body evangelist** — More than any other major creator, Ethier consistently advocates for 3×/week full-body training as the optimal split for most people, citing frequency and time-efficiency research.
- **Before/after comparisons within videos** — His videos frequently test exercise variations side-by-side to demonstrate which produces better activation or better technique.
- **Built With Science ecosystem** — His programs, app (BWS+), and content form a closed loop: the videos teach principles, the programs systematically apply them.

### Recurring Themes & Frameworks

1. **Full-body training 3×/week** — His central programming recommendation: each workout hits every major muscle group, maximizing training frequency while minimizing time commitment.
2. **Exercise-specific technique optimization** — He'll analyze a single exercise (pushup, bench press, squat) for 10+ minutes, testing grip width, elbow angle, tempo, and ROM variations against EMG data.
3. **Research synthesis** — Every video aggregates multiple studies on a single question (e.g., "What builds more muscle — light or heavy weight?") and provides a bottom-line answer.
4. **"Perfect form" as the foundation** — Before discussing programming or nutrition, he emphasizes that suboptimal technique is the most common reason for lack of progress.
5. **Evidence-based time efficiency** — His content implicitly targets busy people who want maximum results in minimum time; full-body workouts and exercise prioritization are central.

- **Visceral fat is more dangerous** — linked to insulin resistance, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome — but also responds faster to diet and exercise
- **Caloric deficit is non-negotiable** — 300-500 kcal below maintenance is the sweet spot for sustainable fat loss
- **Protein is critical during fat loss:** 1.6-2.4 g/kg to preserve muscle mass and increase satiety

- **Soluble fiber** (from oats, beans, fruits) may specifically help reduce visceral fat by improving insulin sensitivity and gut health
- **Alcohol is a triple threat:** empty calories, impairs fat oxidation, and lowers inhibition leading to poor food choices

### Video 5: The Perfect Push-Up To Build Muscle (AVOID THESE MISTAKES!)
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MO10KOoQx5E  
**Duration:** 9:49  
**Views:** N/A (published 2020) | **Published:** November 2020

**Why This Video Matters:** This is the best example of Jeremy Ethier's exercise-specific deep-dive methodology — the format he's most famous for. He takes one exercise (the push-up) and systematically analyzes every variable: hand position, elbow angle, scapular movement, range of motion, tempo, and progression. This video template is what he's applied to dozens of other exercises (bench press, squat, pull-up, etc.).

**Key Takeaways:**
- **Hand position:** Slightly wider than shoulder-width; hands under shoulders at the bottom, not flared out
- **Elbow angle:** ~45-60° from the torso at the bottom — not flared to 90° (shoulder stress) and not tucked to 0° (tricep-dominant, less chest)
- **Scapular movement:** Protract at the top (push through the shoulders, rounding the upper back slightly) to engage serratus anterior and maximize chest contraction. Most people miss this.
- **Full range of motion:** Chest should nearly touch the floor. Partial reps leave gains on the table.
- **Tempo:** 2-3 second eccentric (lowering), explosive concentric (pushing). The controlled negative is where most growth stimulus occurs.
- **Head/neck position:** Neutral spine — don't crane the neck up. Eyes looking slightly ahead of the hands.
- **Core engagement:** Posterior pelvic tilt (tuck the tailbone) and brace the abs. This protects the lower back and makes the push-up a full-body exercise.
- **Progression path:** Standard push-up → deficit push-up (hands on blocks for extra ROM) → band-resisted → weighted (plate on back) → decline (feet elevated) → one-arm progression
- **Common mistakes:** Flared elbows (shoulder impingement risk), sagging hips (lower back strain), half reps (reduced chest stimulus), hands too far forward (excess shoulder stress)
- **The push-up is not just "bench press on the floor"** — the free-moving scapula and core demands make it a fundamentally different and valuable exercise

**Books/Studies/Citations Referenced:**
- EMG studies on push-up variations and chest/triceps/serratus activation
- Shoulder biomechanics research on impingement risk at different elbow angles
- Scapulohumeral rhythm research
- General hypertrophy principles applied to bodyweight training

## Synthesis: The Jeremy Ethier Training Philosophy

Jeremy Ethier's approach can be summarized in five principles:

1. **Full-body training is optimal for most people** — 3×/week full-body workouts maximize frequency, time efficiency, and metabolic demand. This is his most consistent and defining recommendation.

2. **Technique is the prerequisite to everything** — Before changing programs, diet, or supplements, fix your exercise technique. Small form adjustments (hand position, elbow angle, ROM) produce disproportionate results.

3. **Research should inform, not dictate** — EMG studies tell you which muscles are activated, but real-world factors (loading capacity, joint stress, sustainability) determine which exercises you should actually do.

4. **Fat loss is systemic and requires a complete approach** — Calories, protein, resistance training, NEAT, sleep, and stress all matter. You can't out-train a bad diet, and you can't spot-reduce belly fat.

5. **The minimum effective dose is the goal** — You don't need the most complicated program or the most volume. Find the least you can do while still progressing, and build consistency from there.

### Who This Channel Is For
- People who want maximum results in minimal time (45-60 min workouts, 3×/week)
- Beginners and intermediates who need a proven, structured program to follow
- Anyone frustrated by contradictory fitness advice who wants research-based answers
- Lifters who want to optimize specific exercises (push-ups, squats, bench, etc.) with biomechanical precision
- People focused on body recomposition (muscle gain + fat loss simultaneously)
