# The Minimalists (Joshua Fields Millburn & Ryan Nicodemus) — Channel Deep-Dive

## Channel Overview

**Channel:** [The Minimalists](https://www.youtube.com/@theminimalists)
**Identity:** Joshua Fields Millburn and Ryan Nicodemus — childhood friends from Dayton, Ohio, who both achieved conventional "success" (six-figure corporate careers) and found it empty. They walked away from consumer lifestyles to build a philosophy and media empire around minimalism. Authors of multiple books; producers of two Netflix documentaries (*Minimalism* and *Less Is Now*); hosts of The Minimalists Podcast.
**Core philosophy:** The Minimalists define their approach not as deprivation but as intentionality: "Minimalism is a tool to rid yourself of life's excess in favor of focusing on what's important — so you can find happiness, fulfillment, and freedom." Their message is that consumer culture has sold a false promise — that more stuff, more money, more status will make you happy — and that clearing the external clutter reveals what actually matters. They explicitly reject the aesthetic minimalism ("owning 100 things or less") in favor of the functional kind: remove whatever doesn't add value to your life.

## What Makes The Minimalists Unique

1. **The "two friends telling their story" format.** Unlike solo teachers or expert-interview shows, The Minimalists operate as a duo. Joshua (the writer, more philosophical) and Ryan (the "recovering maximalist," more practical) have a conversational dynamic that makes their content feel like eavesdropping on old friends, not being lectured.

2. **Focus on the "why" of consumption, not the "what."** They spend more time exploring why people overconsume (emotional voids, status signaling, the hedonic treadmill) than on decluttering tactics. Their analysis is closer to social commentary than to Marie Kondo's tidying methodology.

3. **The addiction framework.** Both Millburn and Nicodemus frame their past consumer lifestyles as addiction — shopping as compulsive behavior filling the same psychological function as substances. This connection resonates with the growing addiction-awareness in self-improvement culture.

4. **"Love people, use things."** Their most-repeated aphorism (borrowed from an unknown origin): "Love people and use things, because the opposite never works." This captures their philosophy in six words.

5. **Essayistic, literary quality.** Millburn's writing (both in books and in the podcast's essays) often reads like literary nonfiction rather than self-help. This distinguishes them from the more workbook-centric approaches of other minimalism teachers.

## Key Frameworks

- **The "value-added" test:** Does this thing/person/activity/obligation add value to my life? If not, it's a candidate for removal
- **"Spontaneous combustion"** (Ryan's term): the realization that material possessions won't fill the void — often hits after achieving financial success
- **"Packing party"** — a practical exercise: pack everything in your house as if moving; only unpack what you actually use over 21 days
- **The "20/20 rule"** — if you can replace something for under $20 and in under 20 minutes, don't let fear of needing it trap you into keeping it
- **"Minimalism is not about having less; it's about making room for more of what matters"**
- **The five dimensions of value:** Does this contribute to my health, relationships, passions, growth, or contribution? If not, reconsider.

## Most Important Content

1. **"Minimalism: A Documentary About the Important Things" (Netflix, 2016)** — their breakout, touring America discussing the philosophy
2. **"Less Is Now" (Netflix, 2021)** — a tighter, more focused follow-up documentary
3. **The Minimalists Podcast** — ongoing, episode-driven conversations on specific topics (decluttering emotions, minimalist parenting, etc.)
4. **"Everything That Remains" (Millburn's memoir)** — the personal narrative underlying the philosophy

## Influences They Cite

- **Henry David Thoreau** — *Walden*, the original voluntary simplicity text
- **Colin Wright** — minimalist traveler, frequently cited as a parallel thinker
- **Leo Babauta** (Zen Habits) — contemporary minimalist blogger
- **Joshua Becker** (Becoming Minimalist) — parallel figure in the minimalist movement
- **Courtney Carver** (Project 333) — capsule wardrobe minimalist
- **Dave Ramsey** — though more a debt-reduction figure than minimalist, the financial-freedom messaging overlaps

## Relevance to Self-Improvement Research

The Minimalists represent the **material simplification** wing. Their work is complementary to:
- Financial freedom creators (Naval: "wealth is freedom from needing to work")
- Mindfulness/meditation teachers (less stuff = less mental noise)
- Anti-consumerist philosophers (the hedonic treadmill, status anxiety)

They are less frequently cited in the productivity/discipline-focused self-improvement channels but have significant cultural influence through their Netflix documentaries and podcast.
